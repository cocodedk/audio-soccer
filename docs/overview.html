Design draft · 9 November 2026 · for blind and visually impaired players

Audio Soccer

A 5-a-side soccer game you play by ear. Every pass, tackle and shot is a sound you can place, a live commentator calls the match, and TalkBack stays on the whole time.

Sample attack

Your team 0 – 0 Northgate Sample attack · 0:00

Headphones are optional. The 14-second sample uses rough synthesized stand-ins for the real sounds, and your browser's voice for the commentator and guide. It pans sounds left and right and muffles sounds behind you; on speakers the panning mostly blurs, which is why the real game says directions out loud.

    What you do

    You control one player at a time, to start with whoever on your team is closest to the ball. Control moves to a teammate only when the ball is dead or when you ask for it, never in the middle of a run. When it moves, what you hear glides to the new player over a moment. A short sound plays, then the player's name tells you who you are now and a word tells you where the ball is. Your teammates run themselves. There are only six things to do.

    ActionWith the ballWithout the ball
    SteerDribbleRun toward a spot. You keep control while you run there.
    KickStraight up the pitch, never backward. Left or Right angles it 15°. A small aim help only nudges a shot that is already nearly on target into the goal.Nothing
    PassTo the teammate who last called "here!"Nothing
    ChallengeNothingTackle when the ball carrier is in reach. Running into the carrier also tackles, with an automatic "voy!" Otherwise it asks to switch to the teammate closest to the ball.
    AskHear the score, the time, where you are and where the ball is
    PauseOpen the pause menu, an ordinary TalkBack screen

    Controls that work with TalkBack on

    TalkBack takes over single-finger swipes, so normal game controls don't work. Turning TalkBack off isn't an option. The first build tests these three on the tester's own phone.

    Option 1 · first choice

    Finger as joystick

    Rest a finger anywhere. Where it sits from the centre of the screen is the way you run. Double-tap to kick.

    Option 2

    Tilt to steer

    Tilt the phone the way you want to run. TalkBack doesn't touch the tilt sensor. Double-tap to kick.

    Option 3 · always supported

    Keyboard or gamepad

    Arrow keys or a stick to run, one button to kick. The most reliable option, for anyone who has one.

    How it sounds

    You hear the match from your own player's position, always facing the goal you attack.

    Left or rightSpoken. When the ball or the goal moves to another side, one word says where: ahead, left, right or behind. It works on a phone speaker. With headphones you also hear it in that ear.
    Near or farHow fast. The ball's tick and the goal's beacon speed up as you get closer, like a parking sensor. Far sounds are also quieter.
    Ahead or behindBright or dull. Sounds behind you are muffled, which works on one speaker, and the coach calls “behind you!”. Loud moments like the goal roar are capped so they never hurt.

    When a lot happens at once, the lower layers get quieter

    1. BallThe rattle while it moves, plus kicks, boards, posts and net. Never turned down.
    2. Calls"Voy!", footsteps closing in, teammates calling for the ball.
    3. GuideKeeper, coach and goal guide calling short directions from where they stand.
    4. CommentatorWho has the ball, fouls, score, time and the drama.
    5. CrowdRises as an attack nears either goal, groans at a miss, erupts at a goal.

    The commentator

    The commentator is part of the controls. They say what sound effects can't: who has the ball, a foul, the score, the time. Lines are short while the ball is moving, and the longer chat waits for a stoppage. They always duck under the ball, the calls and the guides.

    Important moments jump the queue: a goal beats a shot, a shot beats a foul, a foul beats a pass. A line that's out of date by the time it would play is dropped, never said late:

    0.0 sHolm passes to you. Queued: "Holm finds you on the left…"
    0.3 sYou shoot. Said at once: "Shot!"
    0.3 s"Holm finds you on the left…" Dropped, it's out of date.

    Commentary can be set to full, key moments only, or off. The voice is prepared in advance with offline text-to-speech, so the game needs no internet. English and Persian.

    Never needs sight

    • No step needs sight: first launch, permissions, menus, results.
    • It never asks you to turn TalkBack off and never mutes your phone, so you can't get stuck in silence.
    • The ball is always the clearest sound. Nothing is allowed to bury it.
    • The commentator never lags behind play or talks over what you need to hear.
    • Pause is always one action away.
    • No internet, account, ads or tracking.

    Low-vision players and sighted friends get a simple high-contrast picture of the pitch, like the one above. It never carries anything the sound doesn't.

    Borrowed from blind football

    The rules come from blind football, the real 5-a-side Paralympic sport, which was built around sound from the start. Four outfield players and a keeper per side. A pitch of about 40 by 20 metres with boards along both sides, so the ball rarely goes out. A ball that rattles. Players shout "voy!" when they go for the ball.

    Each team has three guiding voices: the keeper in the defending third, the coach in the middle, and a guide behind the other team's goal. You heard the guide in the sample. The game still feels like a full match, with a stadium, a crowd and a league of made-up teams.

    Build order

    Android first, same build as the chess app. Each step goes to the tester, and their feedback decides the next one.

    1. 0a

      Navigation prototype

      Built. A browser page for a computer, on speakers or headphones, in English or Persian: find a still ball, take it to the goal, beat a keeper. It tests whether the sounds are enough to play by ear. Open the live prototype.

    2. 0b

      Phone controls test

      Throwaway. The three control options on the tester's phone with TalkBack on, on the phone's speaker, plus two vibration cues.

    3. 1

      Penalty shootout

      Five each. Aim and time your shot, or listen to the run-up and dive. Proves the sound, commentator and controls.

    4. 2

      Training pitch

      You, the ball, the goal and the goal guide. Learn to find the ball, dribble and shoot. Plus the sound gallery.

    5. 3

      Full match

      5-a-side against computer teams, with all the voices, difficulty and speed settings, and a league.
